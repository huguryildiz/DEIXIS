## Design Context

### Users

Academic researchers using DEIXIS to search, inspect, compare, and synthesize source-linked literature and research evidence.

They are working with long questions, many source versions, partial access, and evidence whose provenance matters. The interface must support sustained reading and verification rather than resemble a conversational AI product.

### Core Jobs

- Ask or revise a research question without losing prior revisions.
- Distinguish retrieved, screened-in, included, inspected, given-to-model, and cited sources.
- Read the answer as the primary artifact while keeping the process auditable.
- Move from a claim to its exact cited passage, then to the original PDF when available.
- Understand incomplete access, failed operations, stale results, and unverified output without opening developer diagnostics.

### Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, and simple. Status and provenance should be legible without implying that an automated result is independently verified.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a restrained, light-first, editorial research-workspace aesthetic. The page should feel closer to a carefully typeset working paper and evidence desk than a dashboard or chat application. Preserve the established serif display typography, sans-serif interface typography, spacing, color tokens, and dark mode. Add visual cues only where they clarify state, provenance, or action.

Elicit is a density and interaction reference, especially for compact bibliographic metadata, evidence inspection, and progressive disclosure. It is not a visual specification. Retain DEIXIS terminology, provenance boundaries, accessibility, and theme identity rather than copying Elicit branding or component styling.

### Design Principles

- Make research and run states explicit, including failure and uncertainty.
- Keep navigation and evidence inspection easy to scan.
- Use motion only to convey live activity, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Maintain accessible labels and sufficient contrast in both themes.
- Prefer whitespace, type scale, weight, and thin dividers over shadows and decorative containers.
- Show the current outcome and next useful action first; place plans, usage, diagnostics, and technical provenance one disclosure level deeper.
- Separate process from deliverable: the activity timeline explains what happened, while the answer/report remains the primary artifact.

## Elicit-Informed Interface Rules

### Information Hierarchy

- Use a calm document hierarchy, not a grid of equally weighted cards.
- Do not repeat the same title, status, or explanation in adjacent regions.
- Avoid card-inside-card layouts, ornamental gradients, glass effects, oversized icons, and generic dashboard metric blocks.
- Use pills only for compact metadata or state: source scope, model/provider, version, access level, and run status. Ordinary labels remain plain text.
- Keep one run as one compact timeline item. Its plan, steps, usage, and errors expand inside that item rather than becoming parallel cards.
- Dense information is acceptable when alignment and grouping remain clear. Prefer compact rows and progressive disclosure over large low-information surfaces.

### Question Intake and Revision

- Begin with the research question, not an open-ended chat transcript. The primary composer should make source scope, depth, and model choices available without competing with the question.
- Ask one short clarification only when ambiguity would materially change the search or answer scope. Otherwise proceed using an explicit, reasonable interpretation.
- Preserve question edits as revisions. Earlier answers remain visible and clearly labelled as stale rather than being silently overwritten.
- Do not compress found, unique, included, inspected, given-to-model, and cited counts into one progress number. Each describes a different evidence boundary.

### Research and Answer Header

- Before a valid answer exists, the research question may serve as the provisional title.
- A valid answer supplies a concise descriptive title in the answer language. Aim for 15–20 words and never exceed 20 words; do not display the entire multipart question as the permanent heading.
- Reveal a newly arrived answer title with the single restrained typewriter transition. Do not replay it for routine rerenders. Show the complete title immediately when `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled.
- Keep revision, scope, depth, answer model, literature model, and reviewer provenance compact and subordinate to the title.
- The report is the deliverable. Run controls and counts must remain reachable without competing visually with it.

### Answer and Report

- Present the answer as a sectioned research document, not as an assistant chat bubble or a stack of generated cards.
- Open with the direct answer, then use descriptive section headings for the parts of the question. Keep limitations and unanswered aspects visibly distinct from supported claims.
- Place compact citation markers at the claims they support. Activating a marker opens the exact stored passage and source version; it must not jump to an ambiguous work-level record.
- Keep source identity, version, reading depth, and locator available near the citation without repeating a full bibliography after every sentence.
- Distinguish source-stated claims from analyst inference and reviewer verdicts. Visual treatment must not turn structural validation into a semantic-support claim.
- Copy, download, and citation-style controls belong to the report surface and remain secondary to reading.

### Source Lists and Screening

- Make source title and bibliographic identity the first reading line. Put authors, venue, year, version, provider/access, and citation count in a compact secondary hierarchy.
- Keep user selection distinct from model screening. The user's included/excluded/undecided choice must visually outrank the model proposal and retain the user's reason.
- Do not present citation count, venue, recency, or embedding similarity as a quality judgment. Label them as metadata or ordering signals.
- Different versions of one work remain visibly separate when evidence provenance differs. Never hide the version behind a generic PDF badge.
- Abstract-only, PDF available, PDF attached, inaccessible, version uncertain, and removed states require explicit text; color alone is insufficient.

### Source Details and Citation Inspection

- Open citation details on **Plain text** by default. The extracted passage is the readable and highlightable evidence view.
- Place **Plain text | PDF** in one compact segmented control near the source metadata. Keep it in the same position when switching modes.
- The plain-text view shows the cited passage, source version, locator, extraction provenance, and limits. It must not imply that DEIXIS semantically verified support.
- Highlight only the exact backend-located, source-owned citation anchor using a light tint of the DEIXIS accent color. Never guess a span, highlight the whole page, or manufacture precision when an anchor is absent.
- If a stored citation has no usable anchor, explain that it cannot be highlighted and that a regenerated answer is required. Do not silently omit the expected highlight.
- Abstract-only records keep the PDF tab disabled and explain why through an accessible label or tooltip.
- The ordinary passage view may use a narrow side sheet. The PDF view expands to the full available workspace so the document remains readable.

### PDF Viewer

- Render PDFs inside the DEIXIS viewer, not inside the browser's native dark PDF chrome.
- Keep the bibliographic header and **Plain text | PDF** control above the document.
- Use a quiet toolbar with page navigation, current/total page, zoom controls, and download. Controls must have accessible names and visible keyboard focus.
- Open on the cited physical page when the citation provides one.
- Fit the page to the available width by default, preserve aspect ratio, and give the document a neutral paper surface against a subtly differentiated workspace background.
- The PDF viewer must remain usable at narrow widths. Hide or simplify secondary controls before removing page navigation or document access.

### Typography and Density

- Use the established editorial serif for research/report display headings and the established sans serif for interface text, metadata, controls, and source details.
- Large headings may be expressive but must not let an unedited long question dominate several screens. Enforce the bounded generated title instead of shrinking type until it becomes unreadable.
- Use compact metadata text with comfortable line height. Evidence passages need a slower reading rhythm than controls or source rows.
- Preserve real paragraph structure in extracted text while repairing layout-only PDF line breaks for reading.

### Color and Status

- Use existing semantic and theme tokens. Do not introduce one-off colors when an established token expresses the state.
- Green means successfully completed work; amber means partial, fallback, uncertainty, or attention; red means blocking failure; muted means waiting or not run; the accent color means live work or the current selection.
- A phase containing a failed or outcome-unknown operation must not appear wholly successful because a later phase completed.
- Highlight tint, selected tabs, links, and live indicators use the theme accent consistently in light and dark modes.

### Motion

- Motion communicates a state change or live activity; it is not decoration.
- Use one high-value transition at a time. The generated-title typewriter effect is the signature answer-completion transition; do not add competing flourishes around it.
- Keep ordinary feedback around 100–300 ms and larger entrances restrained. Avoid bounce, elastic motion, continuous ambient movement, and layout-shifting animation.
- Animate opacity and transforms where possible. Always provide a reduced-motion alternative.

### Responsive and Accessible Behavior

- Validate changed screens at desktop and narrow/mobile widths. When color or hierarchy changes, check light and dark themes.
- Preserve keyboard operation, visible focus, semantic tabs/disclosures, and meaningful status text.
- Do not truncate evidence-critical titles, passages, locators, failure reasons, or version labels without an accessible reveal path.
- Adapt hierarchy on small screens rather than merely shrinking it: metadata may wrap, toolbars may simplify, and secondary diagnostics may collapse, but evidence access remains available.

## Implementation Guardrails

- Inspect existing components and design tokens before adding styles. Prefer the smallest reversible change.
- Preserve current stack and semantics; do not add a UI dependency for local styling or a one-off interaction.
- Reuse the same source metadata and provenance language across source rows, passage details, PDF view, report references, and exports.
- A visual state must derive from recorded backend state. Never create reassuring UI copy that outruns the evidence contract.
- After a UI change, run the web build and relevant browser tests. Add a focused backend test when the design depends on a changed API or validation contract.
