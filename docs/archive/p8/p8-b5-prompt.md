## Why

After B4 nothing in DEIXIS follows a research after its discovery run. B5 builds the follow-up ("watch") data model and the manual check: the owner switches a watch on for a research, the first check silently records what the providers return now (the baseline), and every later "Check now" re-reads the frozen search queries or the works citing the included sources and lists the records that are new to this research, exactly once. Nothing enters the library, the corpus or any count. The scheduler and the catch-up are B6; the screens are B7.

6. **Runs, budget and the send gate on every HTTP request** (section 15 item 4). A check is a `watch_check` run with budget `{"max_provider_requests": WATCH_MAX_REQUESTS, "max_records": WATCH_MAX_RECORDS}` and `target = {"watch_id", "check_id", "deadline_at", "request_hash"}`. `common.send` resends inside the adapter after sleeping (`providers/common.py:160–201`) and PubMed sends ESearch then EFetch (`providers/pubmed.py:143`, `:170`), so a gate only around the adapter call would miss sends. The watch therefore gives every provider call (dispatch and the direct `citing_works`) a **watch-scoped `httpx.AsyncClient` whose transport gates and reserves each actual HTTP request** and delegates it to the app's client (`self.deps.http.send(request, ...)` or an equivalent that keeps the injected test transport working). Before each request the transport, in this order: runs `_checkpoint(run_id, run["scope_revision"])` logic without raising through the adapter (a stop is recorded and the request refused); checks the research is visible, the watch is enabled and its for a `protocol_queries` watch only, its `protocol_record_id` is still the current protocol of `scope_revision` (a `citing_works` watch binds no protocol and is not stopped by a protocol revision; test a citing check on a research that has a protocol, while a new protocol revision is frozen); checks `usage.provider_requests < max_provider_requests` and the deadline; then writes `add_usage(run_id, "provider_requests")` (committed) **before** delegating, so a crash or an unknown outcome keeps the reservation. A refusal raises a dedicated `WatchSendRefused(httpx.ConnectError)` (which `common.send` classifies as not sent, `before_send`) and records why on the read state; the watch maps that outcome to `deferred_budget`, `deferred_deadline` or a stop, never to a provider failure. A stop (pause, cancel, scope change, watch disabled or protocol changed) ends the read loop: pause/cancel/scope use the existing run transitions (`_checkpoint`), a disabled watch or changed protocol cancels the run with `watch_disabled` / `watch_protocol_changed`; nothing is announced. Budget, records and deadline refusals end the current and every later unit with `deferred_budget`/`deferred_deadline` and go to completion (nothing is cut silently). Rate-limit retries are passed as `min(WATCH_RATE_LIMIT_RETRIES, left - 1)` as a hint only; the transport is the enforcement. Resume never resets usage or the deadline. Tests (fake transport that holds requests): a rate-limit resend inside `common.send` refused by the budget; PubMed's EFetch refused after ESearch took the last request; a deadline passing between two internal sends (fixed clock); the watch disabled, the scope revised and a pause requested while a request is held, each refusing the next internal send; a crash between two internal sends then recovery: `usage.provider_requests` equals the requests the fake transport saw.

8. **Baseline per unit, fixed cut, bounded window (owner-level)** (design "Baseline", section 15 items 5 and 10). The watch only ever looks at the first `WATCH_PAGES` pages of each unit in its frozen order (date-descending for OpenAlex units, the provider's own order otherwise), so the **baseline window of a unit is that same depth**: its first `WATCH_PAGES` pages read at the cut. Section 15 item 5's "page limit" is read as a stop before that window was read (a budget, record or deadline refusal, an error or an unknown outcome), not as the window's own depth; records deeper than the window are outside every later read too (for a date-sorted unit they are older than the oldest date reached; for a relevance-sorted unit a record that later rises into the window is announced, which is the stated `coverage_unknown` limit). `baseline_json` maps `unit_key` to `{"state": "pending"|"partial"|"complete", "cut", "cursor", "pages_read", "contract_id", "adapter_revision", "success_boundary", "covered_back_to"}`. A `pending` unit whose first page reads OK gets `cut` = that check's `requested_to`; records of the baseline read go to the seen set with origin `baseline`, no item. The window's equal depth rests on the frozen page size of decision 4: a baseline page is never asked smaller than later pages. This is an explicit amendment of section 15 item 5 (its "page limit" read as a stop before the window was read), agreed with the plan reviewer and recorded as such in the decision. The unit becomes `complete` when its window was read (all `WATCH_PAGES` pages, or the list ended) without error; after a stop, error or unknown outcome it stays `partial` with the cursor of its last succeeded page, and the next check continues **from that cursor** (same query, same cut), never from page one, for the pages the window still lacks; a partial unit does no fresh first-page read in that check. In a continuation read: a record whose provider publication date (decision 10) is known and later than the cut is treated as an ordinary record (announced if new); a record with a known date at or before the cut joins as `baseline`; **a record without a publication date cannot be placed before or after the cut**: it joins with origin `baseline_undated`, no item, and is listed by title in the check's read model under "joined the baseline without a known publication date; some may be newer than the cut" with its count, so it is visible, not silent. A stored cursor is used only when the recorded `contract_id` and `adapter_revision` equal the current ones; otherwise the unit restarts its baseline from the first page with a new cut, recorded as `baseline_restarted: adapter_revision_changed` (seen rows are kept). A provider that failed or was skipped at the first check has no baseline yet; its first successful read later is its baseline. `success_boundary` (per unit) moves to the check's `requested_to` only when the unit's read in that check finished without error; a failed, deferred, skipped or unknown read leaves it where it was, and the check stores the unread window for that unit (`requested_from`..`requested_to`) in `observed_json`. `watches.last_success_at` is set only when at least one unit read succeeded and is a convenience field; the read model shows the per-unit boundaries. Tests: a second baseline page that succeeds with a non-null next cursor completes the window; a baseline stopped after page 1 by the budget continues from page 2's cursor at the next check with the old cut; in that continuation a dated post-cut record is announced, a dated pre-cut record is not, an undated record is `baseline_undated` and listed.
9. **Citing roll.** The citing units of a check are the next `WATCH_CITING_SOURCES` OpenAlex ids, in decision 3's order, from the stored roll position (in `baseline_json["_citing_position"]`), wrapping; the check records `rolled_over` (how many included works with an OpenAlex id were not read this time) and the next position. The position moves only at completion. One selection algorithm: take the citing units whose baseline is `partial`, in decision 3's order, then the units from the roll position onward, wrapping; drop duplicates; keep the first `WATCH_CITING_SOURCES` of that list (the cap covers both kinds together); the roll position moves past the last roll unit taken. Test with more partial units than the cap and with a wrap. A work that leaves the included set drops out of the units; a new included work gets its baseline on its first read.
10. **Records, aliases and dates** (section 14 item 1, section 15 items 3 and 11). Per read record, `check.py` builds `{provider, provider_record_id, doi, merge_by_doi, title, authors, year, publication_date, publication_date_source, version_time, version_label, publication_type, abstract, identifiers, landing_url, retrieved_at, aliases, relations}`. **Aliases name one version** and decide identity: `<provider>:<provider_record_id>` (arXiv's id keeps its version, e.g. `arxiv:2301.00001v2`); `doi:<normalized>` **only when `merge_by_doi` is true and the DOI is not a version-family DOI** (a DOI under `store.ARXIV_DOI_PREFIX` is a family DOI whichever provider returned it: OpenAlex marks every record `merge_by_doi=True`, so the flag alone does not protect arXiv versions); from `identifiers`: `openalex:<W id>`, `pmid:`, `pmcid:`. **Relations are not aliases**: a family DOI (`merge_by_doi` false, or an arXiv-prefix DOI) is stored as relation `version_family_doi`, and `identifiers.published_doi` as relation `names_published_doi`; relations never make two records one, they feed the `may_be_version` annotation of decision 14e (with `classify_pair(..., names_published_doi=...)`). Never a title. `publication_date` is the exact ISO date only when the provider returned one (`openalex.publication_date_of` on an OpenAlex or citing record); every other provider gives `null` with `publication_date_source: "not_returned"`, and `year` stays apart: a date is never derived from a year. The confirmed version time is not available from any of these reads and is stored as `version_time: null`, never guessed. `first_seen_at` is the local time the seen row was created. A record whose only alias is its provider-native id is `identity_uncertain`. Tests: arXiv v1 and v2 sharing one DOI stay two seen rows (v2 is announced with a `may_be_version` relation to v1), an arXiv record naming a published DOI and the published record stay two rows with the relation recorded, an OpenAlex and a bioRxiv record of one `W` id are one row (a provider-native id names that provider's record, which is how the library itself matches, `store.py:1200–1205`; the decision states that an OpenAlex work can hold several locations and that the watch, like the library, treats it as one record); an OpenAlex record carrying an arXiv family DOI meets stored arXiv v1 and v2 in the library and in the seen set as relation `version_family_doi`, never as one identity.
11. **Scope binding** (section 14 item 2). A watch stores `scope_revision` and `protocol_record_id` (null for `citing_works`, which still binds `scope_revision`). It "follows an old scope" when the research's current scope revision differs or, for `protocol_queries`, the current protocol record of that revision is not the bound one; the read model says so, and Check now answers 409 `watch_follows_old_scope`, and the check's read model shows it was refused. `POST .../watches/{id}/rebind` disables the old watch and creates a new one of the same kind on the current scope and protocol, with a fresh baseline and its first check, in one transaction (seen set kept: it is research-level). A scope change while a check runs cancels the check at the next gate (decision 6) with nothing announced; test it with the revision committed while a fake provider holds a request.
12. **Completion is one transaction** (design "Atomic queueing and completion", section 14 item 2, section 15 item 9). After the last unit, the branch runs `complete_check(...)` in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, which first re-checks the **live state** the reads depended on: the run is `running` or `pause_requested` (a pause asked during the last request lets the completion go on: nothing is left to pause, as `_execute_run` treats any run); the research is visible; the watch is enabled; the research's current scope revision is the check's and, for `protocol_queries`, the current protocol record is the watch's; the watch's `state_version` equals the check's. On any mismatch nothing is published: the run is cancelled with `watch_disabled`, `watch_protocol_changed`, `scope_revised` or `research_unavailable`, or failed with `watch_state_changed`, the reads stay recorded, and the transaction commits only that. Otherwise it reads every `watch_reads` row of the check in persisted order; applies decision 14; writes new seen rows and aliases, merges, new items, item kind changes and the `found_by_json` additions; updates `baseline_json` (and `state_version`), the citing position, `last_checked_at`, `last_success_at`; sets `observed_json`, `provider_status_json`, `counts_json`, `completed_at`; finishes the `watch_complete` step `succeeded`; moves the run to `completed`, or to `failed` with `no_provider_read` when no unit read succeeded (section 14 item 3: a check that made no request, or whose every request failed, is never `succeeded`); writes the events (`watch_check_completed` with counts). A completion that finds its step already `succeeded` writes nothing. Tests are kept apart by kind: (a) an exception inside the transaction rolls everything back (no seen row, item, baseline change or terminal state written by it), and the worker then records `failed`/`internal_error`; a failed run is not resumable (`store.py:768–780`) and the decision says so; (b) a process interruption is simulated by `Worker.recover` on the running run after the reads and before completion: the run is `paused` (`backend_restarted`), resume completes it once with no new request; (c) an interruption after the completion commit: a re-execution writes nothing and sends nothing; (d) a payload file write that fails (`OSError`) leaves no `watch_reads` row and no succeeded page step; (e) each live-state change (watch disabled, scope revised, protocol revised, research trashed) committed while the last request is held gives the named terminal state and no seen row or item.
13. **Content-bound idempotency and expected revisions** (section 15 item 13). Header `Idempotency-Key` (1–200 characters) on every write route. The request hash is SHA-256 of canonical JSON over the route name, the research id, the watch or item id and the request body. Bodies carry the expected revision the user saw: create `{kind, mode, expected_scope_revision}` (409 `scope_changed` on mismatch); Check now, disable and rebind `{expected_state_version}` (409 `watch_changed`); dismiss `{reason, expected_status}` (409 `item_changed`). Storage: create on `watches` (`idempotency_key`, `request_hash`); Check now on `watch_checks` (`request_key`, `request_hash`); rebind on the new watch's `idempotency_key`/`request_hash`; disable on `watches` (`disable_key`, `disable_hash`); dismiss on `watch_items` (`dismiss_key`, `dismiss_hash`). An exact replay writes and sends nothing and is answered **before** any expected-revision or other check, so a replay after later state changes (another check, a disable, a rebind, a later dismissal) still succeeds. The replay response contract: `{"replayed": true}` plus the stable identifiers the original command created or acted on (watch id, check id and run id, item id) and the **current** read model of those resources (not a frozen copy of the first response); the first response has the same shape with `"replayed": false`. Tests assert identifiers equal and `replayed` true; the same key with other content is 409 `idempotency_key_reused`. Tests per route: exact replay after a later state change, and changed-content reuse.
14. **Announcement** (design "Seen set and identity", "What is announced", section 14 item 1, section 15 item 3). For each record of the check, in read order:
    a. **Exact alias first.** If any alias is in `watch_seen_alias` of the research, it is that seen row (following `merged_into`); aliases it brings that are not stored are added to that row (a DOI arriving later on a known provider id is not new). **Late metadata**: if the record now classifies differently by `notice_type` than the seen row did (for example a later title makes it a retraction notice), the seen row's `record_json` keeps its first copy and appends the new classification with the check id; an existing item keeps its id, `status` and dismissal and changes `kind` (`new_record`→`notice` or back) with an entry in `kind_history_json`; no second item is created; a seen row without an item (baseline, in library) gets no item. If its aliases hit two or more different live seen rows, the rows are merged: the oldest row (by `created_at`, then `id`) stays the key, the others get `merged_into` and their aliases move to it; if more than one of them has an item, the oldest item (same tie-break) stays and the others get `status='merged'` and `merged_into_item_id`, keeping their dismissed fields; nothing is announced. An exact DOI or provider-id match decides before any similarity rule (section 14 item 1: `classify_pair` would never merge two published records with one DOI).
    b. **Library, exact only.** Else, if any alias matches the library exactly **through `identifier_mappings` only** (scheme `doi` for a `doi:` alias, a scheme the store writes only for a merge-eligible DOI, `store.py:1454–1458`; the provider scheme for `<provider>:<id>`; `openalex`, `pmid`, `pmcid` likewise), never through `source_versions.doi`, which also holds family DOIs (`store.py:1361–1367`), the record joins the seen set with origin `in_library`, no item, and every matched `source_version_id` (sorted; several versions are recorded as several, never one chosen) is copied into `record_json` (informational; watch rows never reference library rows by foreign key).
    c. **Notices.** Else, a record that `record_identity.notice_type` classifies is seen with origin `notice` and an item of kind `notice` (unless in baseline mode): notices are listed apart and never counted as new papers.
    d. **Baseline.** Else, in baseline mode (decision 8): seen with origin `baseline`, or `baseline_undated` for an undated record in a continuation read (decision 8), no item.
    e. **New.** Else it is new: a seen row with origin `announced` and an item `new_record`, `status='new'`. Similarity never merges and never suppresses: `classify_pair` is run against the research's live seen rows that have an item or origin `baseline`/`in_library` and against the research's corpus source versions; every non-None verdict is stored in `relations_json` as `{"relation": "may_be_version"|"notice_of"|"artifact_of", "against": "seen"|"library", "id", "title", "link_kind", "rule"}` (`notice_of` and `artifact_of` verdicts are kept distinct from "another version"), together with decision 10's relations and the item is still announced, shown in B7 as "may be another version of …" (section 15 item 3: similarity is never automatic proof of identity). This deliberately departs from design section 7, which let an automatic-merge verdict count as "already seen" or "already in library"; the decision states the departure. Whole-library comparison is by exact alias only; similarity runs against the research's own corpus and seen set, which bounds the cost; the decision says so.
    **Relations are reconciled for every record that has or gets an item**, including a new notice (branch c) and an alias hit whose seen row has an item (branch a): the `classify_pair` comparisons of branch e run for it, and new relations are appended to the item's `relations_json` with the check id (append-only; an existing relation is not duplicated); the item keeps its id, status, dismissal and `kind_history_json`. Because a verdict is symmetric, after all records of the check are processed every relation found is also written **on the counterpart's existing item** when the counterpart is a live seen row with an item (dismissed or not, whether or not this check returned it), with the counterpart's side of the verdict (`notice_of` on a notice when its paper arrives, `may_be_version` on both versions). Tests: a notice announced in one check whose paper arrives in a later check that does not return the notice again: the paper's item gets the relation and the earlier (also dismissed) notice item gains `notice_of`, keeping its dismissal.
    A record found by both kinds, or by two units, is announced once per research (`UNIQUE(research_id, seen_id)`); the later finder is appended to `found_by_json`. Counts per check: `records_read`, `returned` and `dropped` per unit, `new`, `notices`, `already_seen`, `already_in_library`, `baseline`, `may_be_version`, `baseline_undated`, plus the watch totals `new_open`, `dismissed`, `added` (always 0 in B5).
