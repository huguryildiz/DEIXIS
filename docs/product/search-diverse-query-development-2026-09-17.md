# Diverse short queries: bounded development probe

**Status:** protocol frozen before generating or running the four-query arm below. The six LitSearch questions have already been used in earlier experiments, so every outcome here is development evidence only.

## Comparison

Use the same six cases in `scripts/search_method_validation_cases_2026-09-17.json`. The baseline is the saved Semantic Scholar first 100 from `.local/search-method-validation-2026-09-17/`, generated by one compact core-plus-facet query. For the new arm, one tool-free `gpt-5.6-luna` medium call per question creates exactly four distinct plain-language searches, each one to five words, based on the question alone. No gold DOI, title, saved result, or failure description enters the prompt. Search each query in Semantic Scholar for at most 25 records (four requests, at most 100 returned records). Do not adapt queries to search results. Preserve raw provider payloads, hashes, status, retries, and exact model identity. Use a separate ignored runtime directory and no DEIXIS service or library.

## Scoring and limits

The unit is a question. Count exact-DOI gold works in the saved baseline and the DOI-deduplicated union of four searches. Report gold works found of 12, questions with at least one gold work of six, unique DOI counts, and provider failures. The new arm is promising if it adds at least one gold in at least two previously missed questions without losing coverage in either baseline-hit question. The probe does not establish scientific relevance beyond incomplete benchmark gold labels. It changes query count and ranking exposure while holding maximum returned records constant, so any gain does not identify which mechanism caused it. Validate any promising arm on fresh questions and engineering topics before a product change.
