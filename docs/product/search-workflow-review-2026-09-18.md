## SW1 — Code decides literature screening; the model only proposes, and only outside the clear cases

**Status:** accepted as design direction; not implemented in the product. Measured once, in an isolated `.local/` run. **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** The quantum end-to-end entanglement screening marked 119 of 160 work groups `provisional_include`, 111 of them from the abstract alone. A full-text adjudication (`.local/quantum-work-adjudication-2026-09-18/`, `REPORT.md`) obtained readable text for 78 groups. Of the 54 prior includes that could be decided, 13 formulate no variables, objective and constraints; they are heuristics evaluated by simulation. The designated negative control `10.3390/e27060615` is one of them and says so itself: "For this reason, we do not choose the optimization approach instead of the heuristic one." Abstract words such as "optimal", "routing" or "LP" did not establish a formulation. 82 groups had no obtainable full text and stay undecided.

**Decision:**

1. Decision authority is code. A model may propose synonyms, a label with one exact quote, and a rationale sentence. Code verifies every quote verbatim in the abstract or stored page text (whitespace, ligature and line-break hyphenation normalised) and derives the final status from a rule table. The model's confidence is never used. With the model switched off the flow still runs; only the unresolved and human-review counts grow.
2. An abstract never yields `include`. Abstract screening has three outcomes: candidate, out of scope, unresolved. Inclusion needs full text with verified anchors for the claim itself. Missing or unreadable full text is `unresolved`, never "no".
3. Search is broad, the decision narrow. Queries are compiled by code from three or four concept blocks: (1) setting/object and (2) task/phenomenon are mandatory and are the only gate; (3) decision/method family is queried broadly; (4) outcome/metric is optional and otherwise a ranking boost. Words of the claim being tested (here ILP, "subject to", MDP) and exclusion words (survey, neighbouring domains) stay out of the query; they are used for ranking, passage extraction and out-of-scope flags. A model-suggested term enters the vocabulary only if it occurs in at least one verified positive. Each term's yield (records, verified positives) is recorded.
4. Ranking uses four signals without embeddings, fused by rank with RRF: BM25 against the question and vocabulary, concept-block coverage, TF-IDF cosine to verified seed papers, and citation-graph similarity (bibliographic coupling and co-citation). An embedding signal is optional, low-weight, rescue-only: it may add a record to the inspection list, never gate or include one, and is switched off if it contributes no verified positive.
5. Code first, model only where code cannot close the case. On the 78 full-text groups a strict-cue gate (`code_gate.py`: at least one "subject to", three "constraint", and one variable/objective/program-family cue) closed 31 groups as "formulation present"; all 29 of those the full run had decided agreed, none contradicted. "No cue at all" (12 groups) is not a negative: the model found a formulation in 6 of them (prose or SDP formulations). Those 12 and the 35 middle-zone groups go to the model.
6. A model label outside the clear zone counts only if two independent runs agree. Re-running the same model (`gpt-5.6-luna`, Codex connection, effort medium) on identical input for those 47 groups changed the yes/no/unresolved answer in 5 (11%: g004, g060, g105, g129, g149); `model_family` matched in 34 of 47 and feature labels in 72–100%. Disagreement goes to the human queue.
7. Undecidable records are not forced. Contradictions with a prior label, unverifiable quotes, non-standard model families and preprint-only evidence go to a human queue with quote and page attached. Versions of one work are one evidence unit.
8. Every search or signal is measured against a probe set of verified positives and known negatives (positives found, negatives promoted). Discovery stops when two consecutive rounds each add under 5% new verified records.

**Limits:** One topic, one model, 78 full texts; 38 of the 51 confirmed formulations were read as arXiv preprints, not publisher versions. The comparison target is the pipeline's own output, not a human-approved gold standard, so the agreement figures measure consistency, not accuracy. Verbatim verification shows that a quote exists, not that it supports the label. The gate thresholds were chosen by hand on this data and are not validated elsewhere. The code-first gate saved 49 of 126 model calls and about 43% of tokens on the full-text groups (1.99M to 1.14M), before the second confirming run required by point 6, which would spend most of that saving again. Whether the decision must also be *operational* (three placement/design papers, g008, g029 and g122, have a verified formulation) is a separate criterion that is not yet checked. Citation-graph similarity, block coverage as a gate and per-signal yield measurement do not exist in the product.

## SW2 — Search keywords come from code first; the model is a conditional expansion step

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** First question of the workflow review after SW1: who determines the search keywords derived from the user's question. Code alone cannot know synonyms that are absent from the question; a model alone is not repeatable (SW1 measured an 11% answer change on identical input), can invent plausible terms the field does not use, and tends to put the claim under test into the query.

**Decision:**

1. Language: code detects the question language. If it is not English, terms (not the sentence) are translated; the simplest accepted route is an English key-term field in the interface. Any translated term must pass the record-count probe in step 2.
2. Keyword extraction is code: strip the question frame, extract noun phrases, drop phrases that are frequent in general language, and probe each phrase with a count-only OpenAlex request (zero records: drop; very large count: usable only inside an AND). Claim words and exclusion words go to the side lists of SW1 point 3. This vocabulary works on its own.
3. The first search is compiled and run from that vocabulary by code.
4. Expansion comes from data first: author keywords and title n-grams of the first-round candidates.
5. A model is called only when the first round is thin or the user asks. It proposes a term list, never a query; its terms pass the same count probe and do not stay unless they occur in a verified positive.
6. The vocabulary is shown to the user with each term's origin (question, data, model), corrected, hashed and frozen. The model is not asked again within the same research.
7. Queries are compiled by code: OR inside a block, AND between blocks, per provider syntax.

**Limits:** Not implemented and not measured. A first query built only from the question text can be narrow; if the user did not write the field's common term, step 4 has little to learn from, which is why step 5 stays as a conditional step and is not removed. Assigning phrases to concept blocks by rule is unreliable and is left to the user's confirmation. The count probe shows that a term exists in the literature, not that a translation is correct. The current product takes the concept vocabulary from the `search_plan` model step ([D44](../decisions.md)), so this entry describes a change of order, not current behavior.

## SW3 — OpenAlex is the search backbone; other sources are conditional and block terms start broad

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured once in `.local/quantum-source-comparison-2026-09-18/`. **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** Second question of the workflow review: which database is the main one and how sources combine. The same frozen three-block query (setting AND task AND decision, title/abstract) ran on OpenAlex, Scopus and IEEE Xplore with no model call; the probe set was the 31 high-confidence full-text-verified positives of SW1. Found: OpenAlex 17, Scopus 17, IEEE 15, union 23 of 31 in 890 distinct DOIs. Positives only one source found: OpenAlex 4, IEEE 2, Scopus 0. Of 161 DOIs that Scopus or IEEE returned and the OpenAlex query did not, 39 are in OpenAlex without an abstract, 12 are absent from OpenAlex and 110 have an abstract there. Widening the task block from the found positives' own titles and abstracts (n-grams, document frequency at least 3, OpenAlex count probe; 3 of 17 phrases accepted) recovered 1 of the 8 held-out misses for 119 more records. Replacing the task phrases with root words (`entanglement`, `entangled`, EPR/Bell pair) recovered 3 of 8, union 26 of 31 in 1,263 DOIs. Semantic Scholar bulk search with the root-word blocks returned 911 rows in one request and found 20 of 31, the same as OpenAlex, and none of the five positives the other three still missed.

**Decision:**

1. OpenAlex is always queried and is the identity, citation-graph and open-access backbone; every other source's records are attached to it by DOI.
2. A domain source is added by code from the field distribution of the first OpenAlex round, not by asking a model: IEEE Xplore for engineering and computing, PubMed for medicine and life science, arXiv for physics, computing and mathematics.
3. Scopus is off by default. In this field it cost about 60 requests per round and contributed no positive the others lacked; its key has no abstract view. It is measured again before use in another field.
4. Semantic Scholar bulk search may run in parallel with the first OpenAlex search as redundancy and as a second citation graph, under the [D67](../decisions.md) pacing gate. It is not counted as a recall gain: here it added no verified positive.
5. Block terms start as root words or broad terms. A phrase replaces a root word only when the count probe shows the root makes the result set unmanageable; code decides from the counts. Narrow phrases lost more positives than any missing source.
6. Citation chaining from the positives of the keyword round is a core discovery arm, not an option: references and citing works from OpenAlex, kept only if their title or abstract passes a broad setting-or-task text filter. Measured here from 26 seeds: 4 of the 5 positives that all four keyword sources missed were reached (three through references, two through citing works), in a filtered pool of 580 works (914 unfiltered) for 114 requests and no failure; keyword search plus chaining reached 30 of 31. Expansion from first-round text (SW2 step 4) stays but is not relied on to close the synonym gap.
7. Every source and every block change reports two numbers on the probe set: positives found and records added. A source that brings no positive of its own over several researches is switched off for that field.

**Limits:** One topic and hand-written blocks, not the code-generated vocabulary of SW2. The probe set was itself discovered through OpenAlex-based runs, so every figure favours OpenAlex, and nothing is known about the relevance of the records only Scopus, IEEE or Semantic Scholar returned. Each source was capped at 1,000 rows; OpenAlex reported 1,366 for the root-word query, so some misses may be cap effects. Five positives stayed missed by all four sources; the reasons were not verified (one uses "quantum switch" and matches no setting term, one probably fails the decision block). Root-word blocks were tried only here and may be far noisier in other fields. How many records Semantic Scholar returned that the other three did not was not computed. The chaining seeds were verified positives, which a real first round does not have yet; chaining from unverified top-ranked candidates was not measured and will give a larger, noisier pool. How many of the 580 chained works were already in the keyword pool was not computed. The one positive chaining did not reach (g102) is a 2025 preprint with no citation links to the seeds.

## SW4 — Citation-chaining seeds are chosen by code, 15 to 25 of them, not three by embedding

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-source-comparison-2026-09-18/` (`seed-count-curve.json`, `realistic-seeds.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** A seed is a paper whose references and citing works are collected so that related work is found without sharing its vocabulary (SW3 point 6). `scripts/isolated_hybrid_search.py::select_seeds` picks exactly three seeds by embedding cosine to the question with a diversity penalty, behind a title gate hardcoded to this topic. Two measurements, OpenAlex only, no model call, one hop. (a) Verified seeds: with k of the 31 high-confidence positives as seeds, the share of the other positives reached was 16–29% at 3 seeds, 25–42% at 5, 37–57% at 8, 55–62% at 15 and 55–64% at 20 (range: 200 random orders versus most-cited first); the pool grew by about 50 works per seed and the curve did not flatten at 3 or 5. (b) Realistic seeds: the first OpenAlex round (1,367 rows, 20 of 31 positives) was ranked by code only (concept blocks hit in the title, then BM25 of question and vocabulary) and the top k unverified records were the seeds. Of the 11 positives that round missed, chaining reached 0 with 3, 5 or 10 seeds and 4 with 15, 20 or 25 seeds, adding 223, 303 and 357 new works. Of the top 25 seeds, 7 were confirmed positives, 4 undecided and 14 never adjudicated; one work held two seed slots as preprint and published record.

**Decision:**

1. Seeds are selected by code from the first-round candidates: blocks hit in the title, then BM25. No embedding and no topic-specific title gate.
2. The seed count is a range, not a constant: at least 15 and at most 25, stopping early only when the last five seeds add almost no new work that passes the setting-and-task text filter. Three or five seeds are rejected: with unverified seeds they reached none of the missed positives here.
3. Seeds are deduplicated at work level (preprint and published version count once) before the count is applied.
4. All chained works that pass the broad text filter go to screening. Ordering by the number of seeds linking to a work is kept as a priority order only: it placed one of the four reached positives in the top 100 and the rest between ranks 150 and 290, so it is not a cut-off.
5. A second ring is conditional: works from the first ring that are verified become the next seeds; chaining stops when two rounds each add under 5% new verified records (SW1 point 8).
6. A paper named or uploaded by the user is always a seed, in addition to the code-selected ones.

**Limits:** One topic, one hop, OpenAlex citation data only; citing works were capped at 400 per seed. In (b) the seeds came from the OpenAlex round alone, so the 11 targets include works another source would have found by keyword. Seed purity and arm precision were measured afterwards (`.local/quantum-chain-precision-2026-09-18/`, code gate plus two agreeing `gpt-5.6-luna` runs, SW1 rules). Seeds: of the 14 never-adjudicated seeds 11 had full text; 9 formulate no model and 2 do, and those 2 are one work in two records, so of the 25 seeds 8 works are verified formulations, 9 are in scope without a formulation, 4 stay undecided and 3 have no full text. Chained works: 331 of 357 resolved in OpenAlex, 88 pass the setting-and-task text filter by code, 57 of those had full text, and 11 of the 57 (19%) hold a verified end-to-end formulation (4 closed by the code gate, 7 by two agreeing runs), 44 do not, 2 had disagreeing runs. Two of the 11 were already known to the ledger, so chaining added 9 new candidate formulations; at least one of them is a review article, which this check does not exclude. The 69 chained works without an abstract and the 31 in-scope works without full text were not judged; the jump from 0 to 4 between 10 and 15 seeds comes from individual seeds in positions 11 to 15 and may not repeat elsewhere. The relevance of the 357 chained works was not assessed, so precision of the arm is not known. Very recent papers have no citation links and stay out of reach of this arm.

## SW5 — Surveys are flagged by text and reference count, moved to the seed pool, and missing abstracts are filled from a second source

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-source-comparison-2026-09-18/` (`survey-metadata.json`, `chain-pool-scope.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** The chaining precision check of SW4 let at least one review article through as a "verified formulation", and 69 of 331 chained works had no abstract in OpenAlex, so code could not judge their scope. Survey signals were measured on 1,336 earlier-screened records with an abstract, against the 133 the earlier model had labelled `secondary_synthesis` (not human-verified). OpenAlex `type = review` marked 2 of the 133 (recall 2%); even *Reviews of Modern Physics* papers are typed `article`. A loose venue-name cue had precision 0.05 and hit 6 prior includes; a hand-picked venue list had precision 0.50, recall 11%, and hit 3 prior includes. Reference count at or above 150 had precision 0.83 and recall 29%; at or above 100, precision 0.55. The text rule (strong title word or a self-describing abstract phrase) had precision 0.72 and recall 62%; title words alone found 22%. Text rule or 150 references: precision 0.72, recall 71%, 2 prior includes hit. Weak title words with 80 references added 3 records.

**Decision:**

1. Survey detection uses title, abstract and reference count only; no full text and no model call. A record is a survey candidate if its title has a strong word (survey, review, overview, tutorial, roadmap, primer, taxonomy, state of the art, systematic mapping, bibliometric), or its abstract describes itself as one ("this survey", "we review", "comprehensive overview", "literature review"), or it cites 150 or more works.
2. Weak title words (recent advances or trends, challenges, perspective, vision, future directions, open problems) never flag a record on their own.
3. OpenAlex `type` and venue names are not used as survey signals.
4. The flag is a routing label, not a deletion. A survey candidate leaves the candidate list, is not sent to full-text adjudication or a model, and enters the seed pool for citation chaining (SW4). If its full text is available and passes the strict-cue gate of SW1 point 5, it goes to the human queue, because a survey may also propose an original model.
5. A record without an abstract is `unresolved`, never out of scope. Before the scope filter runs, code asks a second source (Semantic Scholar, then Crossref) for the abstract by DOI and records where it came from.

**Limits:** One topic. The reference label is a model's earlier judgement, so the precision figures measure agreement, not accuracy; some disagreements may be the rule being right. About 29% of the labelled surveys pass the rule and continue to full-text adjudication. The 150-reference threshold and the word lists were chosen on this data. Semantic Scholar `publicationTypes` was not measured and may be a better metadata signal than OpenAlex `type`. How many of the 69 missing abstracts a second source can supply was not measured.

## SW6 — A DOI match merges; without one, title, authors and abstract text decide together, and only preprint-to-published pairs merge

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-dedup-2026-09-18/` (`title-pairs.json`, `classified.json`, `signals.json`, `s2-links.json`, `unlinked-links.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** Fourth question of the workflow review: identity and deduplication. The proposed scheme was DOI first, then character-trigram Jaccard on the normalised title, "version of the same work" above a threshold when year and authors agree, "suspected" and kept apart below it, no silent merge. It was checked without a model call on the 2,055 distinct DOIs of the SW3 source comparison (OpenAlex, Scopus, IEEE; 16 OpenAlex requests for year and authors, 5 for abstracts, 1 Semantic Scholar batch, 19 Crossref, 1 arXiv). Of 496 different-DOI pairs with title similarity at or above 0.6, 323 share authors; 68 of those involve a record that is not a paper (Zenodo, figshare, Code Ocean and 4TU deposits, "Publisher Correction", "WITHDRAWN"). Of the 255 paper pairs, 200 have title similarity at or above 0.85 and cover 386 DOIs, about a fifth of the pool; OpenAlex held none of the 323 pairs as one work. Among those 200, abstract trigram similarity is at or above 0.8 in 150, between 0.5 and 0.8 in 20, below 0.5 in 8 and unavailable in 22; the 8 are a conference paper or talk and a journal article under one title, or a preprint whose abstract was rewritten. Among the 55 pairs with title similarity from 0.60 to 0.85, 5 have abstract similarity at or above 0.8 (versions retitled at publication) and 38 are below 0.5 (sibling papers by the same authors, such as a "Svetlichny" and a "Mermin" paper, or "connection-oriented" and "connectionless"). Semantic Scholar held 150 of 181 arXiv-to-published pairs as one paper and attached the preprint to a different published DOI in 12, of which 11 lie in the 0.60 to 0.85 title band. For the 19 pairs it left unlinked, plus the two cross-title version groups of the work ledger, arXiv's author-supplied DOI field linked 1 and a Crossref preprint relation linked 1. Of the 150 text-confirmed version pairs, 11 have a year gap of 2 to 4. A first author comparison failed on "Last, First" name order and wrongly separated 6 identical-title pairs. The same DOI carried different title strings across sources in 7 cases, with similarity down to 0.69.

**Decision:**

1. An equal normalised DOI merges two records and nothing else does so alone. Title normalisation strips HTML and LaTeX remnants before comparison. An arXiv DataCite DOI names every version of a preprint and still never merges by itself (`providers/arxiv.py`, `merge_by_doi=False`).
2. Before deduplication, code sets a record kind from the DOI prefix and the title prefix: preprint, published, artifact (data, code, slides), notice (correction, erratum, withdrawal, retraction). Artifacts and notices leave the candidate list and are attached to their parent paper; a withdrawal or retraction is shown as a flag on the parent.
3. Without a DOI match, an automatic "version of one work" merge needs all of: title trigram Jaccard at or above 0.85, author surnames agreeing, abstract trigram Jaccard at or above 0.8, and kinds preprint plus published or preprint plus preprint. If one abstract is missing, an identical normalised title with agreeing authors is enough for the same kinds.
4. An external link is used in both directions. A Semantic Scholar `externalIds` pairing, arXiv's DOI field or a Crossref preprint relation that names the partner confirms a merge even when the title differs; a link that names a different published DOI blocks the merge. Text similarity stays the main signal, because the links covered only part of the pairs.
5. Two published records are never merged. A conference paper and its journal article with agreeing authors and a similar title are linked as `extended_version`; both are kept, they count once in work-level totals, and full-text adjudication prefers the journal article.
6. Every other pair with agreeing authors and title similarity at or above 0.6 is stored as `related_suspected` with its three scores and stays separate. Agreeing authors with abstract similarity at or above 0.8 but a lower title score is `probable_version` and goes to the human queue. Nothing in this step is decided by a model or an embedding.
7. Year is not a gate. A gap above five years blocks an automatic merge; smaller gaps are recorded only.
8. Author comparison uses surnames after handling "Last, First" order, reversed names and diacritics. An empty or placeholder author list ("Anonymous") means unknown, not different, and sends the pair to `related_suspected`.
9. Every merge and link stores its rule, scores and source, and can be undone. Work-level seed deduplication (SW4 point 3) and "versions of one work are one evidence unit" (SW1 point 7) use these groups.

**Limits:** One topic and no human-labelled pairs: abstract similarity and the Semantic Scholar link served as stand-ins for truth, together with reading the pair list, so the counts show agreement between signals, not accuracy. The thresholds 0.85, 0.8 and 0.6 were chosen by hand on this data. Only records with a DOI were in the pool, so DOI-less records, the case that depends most on title matching, were not measured; pairs below 0.6 title similarity were not examined, so versions with a fully changed title are found only through a link, and one of the two ledger groups checked (g047) had none. The kind rule is a short prefix list and will miss other repositories and notice wordings. Whether a journal article may inherit evidence from its conference version, or the reverse, is not settled here. OpenAlex `is_retracted` and Semantic Scholar links for non-arXiv preprints were not measured. The product today merges by DOI only (`providers/common.py`, `merge_by_doi`).

## SW7 — Rank fusion orders the inspection list and never cuts it; every record keeps its four ranks

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-rank-fusion-2026-09-18/` (`fusion-result.json`, `ranks-B.json`, `missing-signal.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** SW1 point 4 names four embedding-free ranking signals fused with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF): the fused score of a record is the sum of 1/(60 + rank) over the signals, so signals with different scales cannot outweigh one another. This had not been tried on the probe set. It was built with no model, no embedding and no extra request on the first OpenAlex round (1,369 records holding 20 of the 31 high-confidence positive works and 14 known negatives): BM25 of the question and block vocabulary, concept blocks hit in the title, maximum TF-IDF cosine to seed papers, and bibliographic coupling plus direct citation with the seeds; a record never used a version of its own work as a seed and ties received the mean rank. With 15 unverified seeds chosen by code (top of BM25 plus blocks; 5 of them confirmed positives), the median rank of the 20 positives was 189 for BM25 alone and 158 for the four-signal fusion, with 6 and 8 in the top 100, 10 and 12 in the top 200, and the worst positive at 746 and 623. With 5 verified positives as seeds (200 random draws, scored on the other 15) the medians were 193 and 121 and the top-100 counts 4.4 and 7.2. Per positive, fusion ranked 11 better and 8 worse than BM25 (sign test p = 0.65). Fusion kept a single failing signal from sinking a record: g016 was 662nd by BM25 and 31st fused; g034 had no reference list, so last in the graph signal, and was 13th fused. The graph signal was the best single signal (median 131 to 150), but 727 records (53%), four positives among them, have no reference list in OpenAlex. TF-IDF to unverified seeds was the weakest: leaving it out moved the fused median from 158 to 121. Leaving a missing graph signal out of the sum instead of ranking it last did not help (median 171). The 14 known negatives had a fused median rank of 225, close to the positives.

**Decision:**

1. The four signals of SW1 point 4 are fused by rank with RRF (k = 60). Raw scores are never added across signals.
2. The fused rank is an inspection order only. It never removes a record and is not a cut-off: every first-round candidate that passes the setting-and-task gate goes to screening, because even the best ordering left 12 of 20 positives outside the top 100.
3. Each record stores its rank in every signal, its fused rank and, per signal, whether the signal was available, so that a position can be explained and yield per signal can be computed.
4. A missing signal (no reference list, no abstract) is ranked last in that signal and recorded as missing. The share of records without a reference list is reported with every ranking.
5. TF-IDF similarity to seeds runs only with verified or user-supplied seeds. With unverified seeds the seed-based text signal is left out; the graph signal stays.
6. Signal yield is measured in every research on the probe set (SW1 point 8): positives in the top 100 and 200 with and without each signal. A signal that does not improve the order over several researches is switched off for that field.
7. Ranking is not expected to separate works with a formulation from works without one; that is decided on full text (SW1).

**Limits:** One topic and 20 positives; the gain of fusion over BM25 is within noise, so the measurement shows that fusion is not worse and protects against one failing signal, not that it is better. The positives are defined by a full-text property the abstract does not show, and no label exists for "on topic without a formulation", so how well the order captures topical relevance was not measured. Co-citation was left out of the graph signal because it needs citing-work requests; the block signal is coarse (title hits, then term count) and heavily tied. k = 60 was not varied. The pool is the OpenAlex keyword round only; chained works and other sources were not ranked. The product fuses only lexical and semantic passage ranks today and has no record-level fusion.

## SW8 — Embedding is a fifth ordering signal with no authority; Gemini on a free key is recommended, a built-in local model is the keyless fallback

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-rank-fusion-2026-09-18/` (`embedding-rescue.json`, `gemini-embedding.json`, `multilingual.json`, `term-query.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-18.

**Context:** SW1 point 4 kept embedding as an optional, low-weight, rescue-only signal. The owner added a product constraint: the people who will use DEIXIS mostly do not hold paid AI subscriptions or API keys, and semantic search today needs a Gemini or OpenAI key or a separately installed Ollama or LM Studio ([D29](../decisions.md)). Both kinds of model were measured on the SW7 pool (1,369 first-round records, 20 high-confidence positive works, 14 known negatives), embedding title plus abstract against the English question. A small open model run locally on CPU with no key (`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` through `fastembed`, 384 dimensions, about 130 MB, about two minutes for the pool on this computer) gave a median positive rank of 85 with 11 of 20 in the top 100; `gemini-embedding-2` with the product's settings gave 73 and 13; the four code signals fused gave 158 and 8 (SW7). In the top 200 the counts were 11 local, 16 Gemini, 12 fused. As an equal-weight fifth RRF signal both reached 10 in the top 100 (median 102 local, 145 Gemini); at weight 0.3 the local signal changed nothing (median 148, 8 in the top 100). The local model ranked two positives 9th and 2nd that the four signals had at 623 and 414 (g044, g062); Gemini ranked two others 44th and 75th that the four signals had at 402 and 552 (g012, g074). Gemini ranked 13 positives above the local model and 7 below. Both also fail on single works (g158: local 740, Gemini 293, four signals 175; g112: Gemini 615). Gemini placed 9 of the 14 known negatives in its top 200, the local model 4: similarity follows the topic, not the presence of a formulation. At the paid rate of $0.20 per million text tokens (Google's pricing page, read 2026-09-18) the 1,369-record pool, about 2 million characters, cost roughly $0.10; the free tier lists text embedding as free of charge, with submitted text usable by Google to improve its products. A follow-up measured the question language on the same pool. With the question translated to Turkish, `bge-small-en` alone fell from a median of 85 to 350 (2 of 20 in the top 100), while Gemini stayed at 64 (12 in the top 100). Two small multilingual local models did not repair this and were weak even with the English question: `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2` had medians of 267 (English) and 370 (Turkish), `multilingual-e5-small` 215 and 426. An English query built by code from terms instead of a sentence was also weak with `bge-small-en`: block terms only 330, block terms plus claim words 274, a key-term list as a user would type it 138, against 85 for the sentence. In every one of these weak cases the five-signal fusion stayed near the four-signal result (medians 155 to 194 against 158; 6 to 9 in the top 100 against 8), so a failing embedding cost little.

**Decision:**

1. Embedding is used, as the fifth ranking signal in the RRF of SW7 with the same weight as the others, and as a rescue arm: records outside the top 200 of the fused four-signal order but inside the embedding top 50 are added to the front of the inspection list with the label "from the embedding arm".
2. Embedding has no authority. It never removes a record, never includes one, and is never a threshold. SW7 point 2 already sends every gated candidate to screening, so this signal changes order only.
3. No step depends on an embedding, and none requires payment. Settings → Semantic search presents the choices in this order: (a) recommended, Gemini `gemini-embedding-2` on a free Google AI Studio key, because it takes the question as written in any language and was the most even model here; the interface shows how to obtain a free key; (b) keyless fallback, a built-in local model for users who want no account or no text leaving the computer; (c) off. OpenAI, Ollama and LM Studio stay as they are ([D29](../decisions.md)).
4. The built-in entry, "This computer · built-in", sits next to Gemini, OpenAI, Ollama and LM Studio. It needs no key, no account and no separate application, downloads the model once after confirmation and shows its size. The provider chosen here also serves passage retrieval in the answer step. Where a free-tier key is in use, the interface states that Google may use submitted text to improve its products, and says so again before a user-uploaded PDF's text is embedded.
5. With the model absent or semantic search off, the flow runs on the four code signals unchanged.
6. The built-in model is English only and is given an English question sentence, not a term list. When the question is not English, the interface asks for an English version of it; a connected model may propose the translation once, the user confirms it, and it is stored with the scope revision. Without an English sentence the built-in arm stays off for that research and the interface says so; a Gemini or OpenAI embedding takes the question as written. The small multilingual local models measured here are not offered.
7. The arm's contribution is reported in every research: records it rescued and verified positives among them. An arm that brings no verified positive over several researches is switched off for that field.

**Limits:** One topic, 20 positives and 14 negatives; none of the differences between the local model, Gemini and the four signals is statistically firm. The free tier's rate limits are not published as numbers and were not measured, deliberately: they change and do not alter the design, since a failed embedding step never stops a run. During implementation one full research is run on a key without billing, and a wait-and-retry on HTTP 429 is added. OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` was not measured. The Turkish question was one translation written for the test, and only two multilingual models were tried, both small; a larger multilingual local model (`multilingual-e5-large`, 2.2 GB, or `bge-m3`) was not measured and may work at a size that is hard to ship. SW2 point 1 (English key terms) does not give the embedding a usable query, so point 6 adds a second English field or a model proposal; how users take that was not tested. Passage-level retrieval with the local model was not measured, so point 4's claim for the answer step is a design intention. The two-minute timing is from one Apple Silicon computer and includes the model load; slower computers and larger pools were not timed. `fastembed` brings `onnxruntime` as a new dependency whose packaging in the desktop build was not checked. The rescue rule's 200 and 50 were picked on this data. The product today scores sources with the provider chosen in Settings and has no built-in local embedding.

## SW10 — Full text is fetched in the background right after the code stage of abstract screening, and one reading step routes by source: arXiv LaTeX first, PyMuPDF text otherwise, Marker only on the pages that need it

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured on 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-19 in `.local/quantum-fulltext-timing-2026-09-18/` (`arxiv-retry.json`, `arxiv-source.json`, `tex-vs-marker.json`, `page-mapping.json`, `other-fields.json`, `scholar-pdf-links.json`, `scholar-pdf-links-all.json`) and from the fetch ledgers of the two earlier full-text runs. **Date:** 2026-09-19.

**Context:** Ninth question of the workflow review: the order of full-text sources (publisher's open-access copy, preprint, Europe PMC, author page, manual download folder), when full text should be read, whether web search helps, what to do about IEEE, and the owner's wish for one reader that gives both text and equations, since Marker is slow ([D52](../decisions.md), [D62](../decisions.md): 60 to 75 s per page) and equations are also needed for the final report. From the two earlier runs: readable text for 78 of 160 works and 68 of 102; nearly all of the 116 without text had no open-access candidate at all; about 5 s and 5 requests per work; median 12 pages and 60,000 characters. Of 146 texts, 102 came from arXiv (about 100 through the PDF address OpenAlex gives, 32 by finding the arXiv twin through an OpenAlex title search), 32 were the publisher's open copy, Europe PMC gave 3 for 118 requests and author or institutional pages 3 to 4. The arXiv API title search had answered HTTP 406 on all 222 requests of both runs; repaired and rerun for the 116 works without text it found 2. OpenAlex marks 980 of the 1,366 first-round records open access (72%). IEEE: 51 of 129 works with an IEEE DOI had text (40%), 39 of them from an arXiv twin and 7 from IEEE's own open-access articles; other publishers 95 of 133 (71%). Web search: Google Scholar through SerpAPI for all 114 works still without text matched 39 titles and gave a PDF link for 16 (14%), 11 on repositories or author pages (NSF PAR 4) and 5 on publisher sites that may be paywalled. LaTeX source: all 30 sampled arXiv texts of this topic have the authors' source (median 1.4 s and 1 MB per paper, median 17 display equations, "subject to" present in 16); in other fields (sensor networks, molecular communication) 23 of 30 (77%), the rest mostly PDF-only records from 2011 to 2015. On the three papers of the D52 equation audit, code extracted 582 display equations; a 40-line expander resolved every custom macro (42 defined in one paper, 19 used in display equations; 168 equations changed by expansion); 62 of Marker's 81 equation lines on the audited pages matched a source equation at 0.85 or more token similarity, and the low matches looked at were artefacts of the matcher (number-only lines, constraints split differently), not source errors. Equation numbers were located on a PDF page through the text layer in every case (8 of 8, 77 of 77, 46 of 46), but numbering source equations by counting failed (11 against 8, 51 against 46, 163 against 77). Of 60 arXiv records, 18 have two or more versions and 23 a journal reference; 15 of those 23 were never updated after the first posting.

**Decision:**

1. Full text is fetched after deduplication (SW6) and the code stage of abstract screening (SW9), in the background and in small batches, one fetch per work, with the version read recorded. Order: papers the user named or uploaded, then code candidates by fused rank (SW7), then `unresolved` records. The user can work with the list meanwhile.
2. Reading has two levels. As soon as a text arrives code extracts it, checks its identity and applies the strict-cue gate (SW1 point 5); this is local and free. A model reads only the middle zone the gate cannot close, in rank order, and stops when two consecutive batches each add under 5% new verified records (SW1 point 8). Records not reached are shown as not read, never removed.
3. Source order stays as proposed: publisher's open-access copy, preprint, author or institutional copy, manual download folder. Europe PMC is queried only in medicine and the life sciences (the rule of SW3 point 2). A PDF the user drops into the folder moves that work to the front of the queue. The arXiv title-search request is repaired (it was refused with 406).
4. Web search is a last resort: only when every other route is empty, only for works near the top of the order, and never with automatic attachment. The found address is shown for the user to check and confirm ([D4](../decisions.md)).
5. IEEE and other closed publishers: first the arXiv or TechRxiv twin (SW6), then an author-accepted copy (other OpenAlex locations, Unpaywall, CORE, funder repositories), then a ranked "waiting for your PDF" list whose links open in the browser through the institution's proxy address set in Settings; a downloaded file is matched to its work by DOI or title. No automated bulk download through a proxy. A work without text stays `unresolved`.
6. Reading is one step for the user and routes by source inside: (a) when an arXiv copy exists, the authors' LaTeX source gives text and equations, with macros expanded, layout commands removed (`\label`, `\nonumber`, `\hspace`, `\setcounter`) and the arXiv version recorded; (b) otherwise PyMuPDF extracts the text, which is enough for the code gate; (c) Marker runs only on pages the code gate marked as holding a formulation and on pages whose equations a report will quote, not on whole papers.
7. An equation taken from LaTeX source is used as a cited equation only when it is matched to the PDF: the page is found through the equation number in the text layer and the source equation is matched to that page by text similarity, not by counting. When the match is not reliable the equation is not taken from the source and that page is read with Marker, as today. The LaTeX route is a speed gain, never a requirement.
8. An equation or passage read from a preprint carries its version label ("arXiv v2"); a published version supplied by the user takes precedence, and versions stay labelled as [D4](../decisions.md) requires.

**Limits:** One topic for the fetch figures, a field with unusually high arXiv coverage; access rates and the source mix will differ in medicine or engineering. The 5 s per work is from one computer and network. The stop rule of point 2 was not tried. The 50 hand-checked equations were not compared with the source one by one against the page images; agreement was measured against Marker's reading, and the matcher was crude. The similarity matching of point 7 was not built or measured; only the page lookup was. Whether equations differ between a preprint and its published version was not measured, only how often a preprint was updated. SerpAPI is a paid service and the web-search gain of roughly 10% real copies came from 114 searches. The proxy flow of point 5 was not tried and cannot be automated or tested without the owner's institutional login. An idea left unmeasured: for an open-access record, skip the abstract-stage model proposal and run the code gate on the full text directly, which could remove much of the roughly 1,900 abstract calls of SW9.

## SW11 — Every record gets one of three answers per stage with a reason code; the human queue holds only real indecision, and each row asks one question with quote and page attached

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Narrows SW1 point 7. Measured in `.local/quantum-queue-design-2026-09-19/` (`apply_design.py`, `design-applied.csv`, `design-stats.json`) on `human-review-queue.csv` of `.local/quantum-work-adjudication-2026-09-18/`, and for the abstract stage in `.local/quantum-abstract-disagreement-2026-09-19/` (`protocol.md`, `run.py`, `result.json`). **Date:** 2026-09-19.

**Context:** Tenth question of the workflow review: the human queue. The proposal was that a record the rules cannot decide is not forced into a class and goes to a review queue (contradiction, unverifiable quote, non-standard case), each row with quote and page ready. The queue of the earlier adjudication run held 140 of 160 works, so it was the list, not an exception list. Of those rows 82 say only "full text not obtained". Of the 58 rows on the 78 full-text works, 12 had "evidence from a preprint only" as the sole reason, 16 named a contradiction with the prior label (mostly an effect of the old abstract-level `include`, which SW1 point 2 removes), and 18 named `model_family = other`, a mixed set (an SDP, nonlinear programs, a two-stage stochastic program, unnamed general formulations, and one mislabelled integer nonlinear program, g132). Evidence readiness: 24 of 563 proposed quotes (4%) were not found verbatim; 519 of 532 verified full-text quotes (98%) carry a physical page number; 17 of the 58 rows have no quote at all, all of them "no formulation" decisions, because an absence has no quote. In 17 of the 19 "no" works the text holds at least one strict cue word, so code can show those sentences with their pages; the gate script stores cue sentences only for its "yes" zone today. The product's `screening` contract has the model propose `include`, `exclude` or `uncertain` from title and abstract. Applying the design below to the 160 works by code, with no model call: 82 wait for a PDF; of the 78 full-text works 46 are `include` (26 closed by the code gate alone, 20 by two agreeing runs with verified quotes), 15 are `criterion_not_met` and 17 (22%) go to the human queue (7 contradictory evidence, 5 disagreeing runs, 4 an include resting on an unverified quote, 4 a criterion part without verified evidence; 3 works carry two reasons). Of the 31 high-confidence positives 28 are `include` and 3 (g009, g033, g042) are queued for an unverified quote; none became `criterion_not_met`. The negative control g072 is `criterion_not_met`. Had they been queue reasons, the preprint-only label would apply to 58 of the 78 works and `family = other` to 20. A second measurement ran the abstract-stage proposal of SW9 point 1 twice on identical input for 100 of the 941 records the code stage leaves to the model (the 60 best by fused rank and 40 of the rest chosen by hash; `gpt-5.6-luna`, Codex connection, effort medium, one record per call, protocol frozen before the first call, 200 calls, none failed). The label changed in 6 of 100, always between candidate and out of scope: 2 of 60 at the top of the order and 4 of 40 in the rest. Of 198 quotes 2 were not verbatim in the abstract. Sending disagreement and failed quotes to a person gives 7 queue rows per 100, roughly 60 to 90 for the 941 records; counting them as candidates gives none and raises the candidates from 78 to 85. The five probe-set positives in the sample were candidates in both runs. The model called 82% of the records candidates, the three known negatives among them, and put 14 to 17% out of scope, for about 2,250 tokens and 4 s per call.

**Decision:**

1. A record has one of three answers per stage, and every answer carries a reason code, who decided, and the next step (the rows of SW9 point 5). Abstract stage: candidate, out of scope, unresolved. Full-text stage: `include` (every part of the research's inclusion criterion has a verified quote with a page), `criterion_not_met` (in scope, but the thing looked for is absent), unresolved. The interface shows three plain states; the reason code serves filters and explanations.
2. `criterion_not_met` is not "out of scope". These works stay visible as their own group in the report and may enter the seed pool for citation chaining (SW4).
3. `unresolved` is split by reason and only one kind reaches a person: no full text goes to the "waiting for your PDF" list (SW10 point 5), not yet read waits in the reading order, no abstract triggers the second-source lookup (SW5 point 5), and real indecision goes to the human queue.
4. The human queue belongs to the full-text stage. At the abstract stage a disagreement between two runs, or a quote code cannot find, does not go to a person: the record counts as a candidate and moves on to full text, because an abstract includes nothing and a wrong candidate costs one reading while a wrong exclusion is a loss. At the full-text stage a record enters the human queue when two model runs disagree (SW1 point 6), when the code gate and the model contradict each other, when an `include` rests on a quote code could not find, when the abstract promises what the full text does not contain, when a part of the inclusion criterion has no verified evidence while other parts do, or when SW5 point 4 or SW6 point 6 send it there. The criterion parts are defined per research (here variables, objective, constraints and end-to-end scope; in another field they differ).
5. Preprint-only evidence and a non-standard model family are labels, not queue reasons. The version label of SW10 point 8 covers the first; the family name is a descriptive field the user can correct at any time and is not part of the inclusion criterion.
6. A queue row asks one question and carries quote, page and version label; selecting it opens the PDF at that page. For a "no" decision code shows the cue counts and the sentences where cue words occur, with pages, or states that no cue was found. For a rejected quote the row shows the model's quote beside the closest passage in the text.
7. The person's decision is final, stored with reason, date and version, and can be undone. The model is not asked again for that record. Until a person looks, the record stays `unresolved`; nothing is forced. The queue is ordered by the fused rank (SW7).
8. Every research reports the queue size and its reason distribution, and presents a small audit sample of records decided outside the queue, because only a person can find wrong decisions there.
9. When the user adds a PDF: code matches it to a work by DOI or title and says which work, version and page count it found; with no match it asks and never attaches the file on its own; unreadable text is reported and the work stays `unresolved`. The work moves to the front of the reading order and the code gate runs at once. The response is one of: closed by code, with quote and page; "the model is reading", then `include` or `criterion_not_met` with verified quotes or cue sentences; or a queue row with its one question. A user-supplied published version takes precedence over a preprint already read, earlier quotes keep their version label, and a different outcome between versions is shown. With the model off, a work the gate cannot close stays `unresolved` and waits. The user may record an own decision at any time.
10. A human decision is stored against the scope revision and criterion it was made under. When the question or the criterion changes, the decision is kept and marked "made under the earlier criterion, look again"; it is never carried over silently.
11. A queue row also offers "not sure" and "this PDF is wrong or incomplete". The record then stays `unresolved` with that reason and does not return to the queue on its own.
12. The source-linked answer rests on `include` works only. The user can ask for an answer before the queue is empty; the report then states the flow counts: included, criterion not met, waiting for a PDF, queued and not looked at, not read.
13. Human decisions feed the probe set (SW1 point 8) and count as verified positives for the vocabulary rule of SW2 point 5. They never retune a threshold or rule automatically. Each research reports how often the person overruled code or the model; that count is the only measure of accuracy the workflow has.

**Limits:** One topic, one model, and no human labels: the figures show that the rules are consistent and how long the queue is, not that the 46 `include` and 15 `criterion_not_met` decisions are right; the comparison target is the same run's own output. The audit sample of point 8 was not tried. The abstract-stage measurement shows consistency between two runs, not whether the labels are right; with 100 records the rates are coarse (4 of 40 is compatible with about 3% to 24%), one prompt was used, and the six disagreeing records were not judged. It also shows that the abstract-stage model removes only about one record in six for about 1,900 calls on this pool, which supports the unmeasured idea at the end of SW10 (skip the abstract proposal for open-access records and run the code gate on the full text); that is left to its own question. Points 10 to 13 were not tried. The minutes a queue row costs a person, the usefulness of cue sentences for "no" rows, the closest-passage matching of point 6, the file-to-work matching of point 9 and whether the responses read clearly to a user were not measured; the only measured parts of point 9 are the share the code gate closes at once (31 of 78 full texts) and text extraction time (about 5 s per work, SW10). The three queued high-confidence positives show the unverified-quote rule is cautious rather than lossy on this data. The status and reason-code names here are working names; the schema is decided at implementation (SW9 point 5).

## SW12 — No reranker and no change of chunk size; passages for the inclusion criterion are found with claim words, not with the question

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-chunk-rerank-2026-09-19/` (`protocol.md`, `run.py`, `result.json`, `cue_rank.py`, `cue-rank.json`, `circularity/`). **Date:** 2026-09-19.

**Context:** The owner asked whether the query structure has a reranker and whether text is chunked. The product has no reranker: passages are ranked by BM25 over SQLite FTS (question words plus the search plan's synonyms, at most 40 terms) and, when semantic search is on, by embedding similarity, fused with RRF (`workflow/flow.py::fuse_rankings`, k = 60); the answer input holds at most 48 passages, and two PDF passages per source when the sources outnumber that limit. PDF text is chunked per page into pieces of at most 1,400 characters cut at paragraph, sentence or line ends, without overlap and never across a page (`documents/pdf.py::chunk_page`). Measured without a model call on the 53 full-text works judged to hold a formulation, with 205 verified quotes as targets and a median of 52 chunks per paper: is a chunk holding a verified quote among the first 2 or 6 chunks of its paper? Chance gives 5 and 14 papers. BM25 on the question gave 6 and 22, `bge-small-en-v1.5` 4 and 18, their RRF 9 and 20. A local cross-encoder (`Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`, 80 MB, 0.8 s per paper) reranking the top 20 gave 2 and 7, worse than chance: it promotes the passages that read like the question, which are introductions and related work, while a formulation passage is mostly equations. Chunking: 12 of 205 quotes (6%) are split by a 1,400-character boundary, 7 with 2,800 characters, none with a 300-character overlap; neither variant ranked better (overlap 6 and 19; 2,800 characters at the same character budget, top 1 and top 3, 6 and 14). A ranker that counts claim-word cues in a chunk ("subject to", "s.t.", maximize or minimize, constraint, decision variable, objective, program family names, sum and inequality signs) gave 41 and 49, median first rank 1. Because those target quotes came from an adjudication in which the model saw only cue-selected passages, the check was repeated with targets free of that selection: `deepseek-flash` (the product's DeepSeek connection, effort high, as the connection has no medium; one call per paper, 1.4 million tokens) read the whole text of the same 53 papers and returned 211 quotes, 195 verified verbatim, 37 of them (19%) outside the earlier candidate passages; it found a formulation in all 53. On these targets BM25 gave 4 and 22, embedding 4 and 18, RRF 8 and 20, the cue ranker 40 and 46 (median first rank 2). Fusing the BM25 and cue orders into one by RRF gave 29 and 40, below the cue order alone.

**Decision:**

1. No reranker is added. It is a ranking signal without authority and so compatible with SW1, but here it lowered the rank of the evidence, and a better one needs either a large local model or a key.
2. Chunk size stays at 1,400 characters within a page, which keeps every passage tied to a physical page. A small backward overlap is allowed at implementation if it is cheap; it rescues quotes cut at a boundary and does not change ranking. Quote verification keeps running on page text, so a chunk boundary never fails a quote.
3. Passage selection uses two queries with separate quotas, not one fused order. Topic passages are ranked as today (question words, optional embedding, RRF). Criterion passages, those that show whether the inclusion criterion is met, are ranked by the claim words of SW1 point 3, which code compiles per research and the user confirms (SW2 point 6). Each source contributes passages from both lists.
4. The same claim-word order chooses the passages the full-text model reads in the middle zone (SW10 point 2), the cue sentences shown on "no" rows of the human queue (SW11 point 6) and the pages sent to Marker (SW10 point 6).
5. Embedding stays what SW8 made it: an ordering signal for records and topic passages, never the way criterion passages are found.

**Limits:** One topic; the target is a formulation passage, which is unusually unlike its question, so nothing here says a reranker is useless for ordinary topical questions, and only one small English cross-encoder was tried. Ranking was measured inside one paper, not in the product's multi-source 48-passage input, and point 3's two quotas were not built or measured. The cue list was written by hand for this topic before the circularity check and not changed after it; how well code can compile such a list for another research (SW2) is unknown. The circularity check removes the passage preselection but not a softer bias: a model asked to quote a formulation will tend to quote sentences that contain these very words. It covered positives only, so that the second model agreed on all 53 says nothing about papers without a formulation. The effect of a wrong or missing cue list is that criterion passages fall back to the topic order, which this measurement puts near chance.

## SW13 — The probe table reports negatives for signals and the gate only; no signal is switched off automatically on a small probe set; discovery stops per arm kind, not on two low rounds in a row

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Refines SW1 point 8 and SW4 point 5. The rules were applied to the data already held. Probe set: 31 high-confidence positives, 51 confirmed in all, 19 in-scope negatives (a heuristic without a formulation) and no verified out-of-scope negative; the chaining check of SW4 adds 7 positives outside the ledger. Arms: the OpenAlex root-word round found 20 of the 31 positives and 14 of the 19 negatives, the OpenAlex block queries 17 to 18 and 12, Scopus 17 to 18 and 5 to 7, IEEE 15 to 17 and 4, Semantic Scholar 20 and all 14 negatives of its pool; the negative count follows the positive count. 3 of the 31 positives (8 of the 51) are in no stored arm. Signals, on the 1,369-record pool with 20 positive and 14 negative groups, top 200: bm25 10 positives and 5 negatives, blocks 8 and 5, TF-IDF to unverified seeds 10 and 7, citation graph 12 and 2, Gemini embedding 16 and 9, the five fused 12 and 5. AUC of positives over in-scope negatives: graph 0.78, embedding 0.75, blocks 0.63, bm25 0.62, TF-IDF 0.49. Leaving one signal out of the five-signal RRF changed the top-200 count by -2 to +1, and in a paired bootstrap over the positive groups (2,000 resamples) every 95% interval contains zero, for the median rank as well and for the 29 confirmed groups as well. Stopping rule, rounds in design order (OpenAlex root words, OpenAlex with data-derived terms, Scopus and IEEE, Semantic Scholar, chaining from 25 unverified seeds): new high-confidence positives 20, 0, 8 (40%), 0, 0; with all confirmed and the chain positives 30, 0, 10, 0, 6. Over the 24 orders of the last four rounds the rule stops early and loses positives in 6 orders (up to 8 of 28) and, for the larger set, in 8 orders (up to 16 of 46); the losing orders put two keyword rounds on similar indexes next to each other before the other arm kinds ran. A two-arm capture-recapture estimate (keyword round against chaining; 20, 16, both 12) gave 26.5 (95% interval 20 to 33) against a known 31, and 36 (28 to 45) against a known 51.

**Decision:**

1. The probe table keeps two numbers per row, but "negatives promoted" is reported for ordering signals and for the code gate only. For a search arm it is not a quality measure: search is broad by design (SW1 point 3), and an arm that finds more positives promotes more in-scope negatives. A search arm reports positives found, positives only it found, and rows returned.
2. Negatives are stored with their kind: out of scope, or in scope with the criterion not met (SW11 point 2). Out-of-scope negatives come from human decisions (SW11 point 13); until some exist the report says that none do, and does not read the in-scope count as a false-positive rate.
3. No ordering signal is switched off automatically by leave-one-out. The report shows each signal's contribution with its interval and the owner decides. The automatic switch-off of SW1 point 4 stays for a signal that costs a key, a model call or a download (the embedding, the model's term expansion) and brings no verified positive that the other signals leave outside the inspection depth.
4. The stopping rule is applied per arm kind: keyword search on a bibliographic index, term expansion, citation chaining. Discovery ends only when every enabled arm kind has run at least once and the last round of each kind added under 5% new verified records. Two low rounds of the same kind say nothing about another kind.
5. The rule counts verified records, and verification arrives later than the round. Until it does the run shows the new-candidate share as a provisional figure and does not stop on it.
6. Capture-recapture between arms is not used as an estimate of what is still missing.
7. Each research reports the probes no arm found, by identifier.

**Limits:** One topic; ledger labels are not human-approved. Keyword rounds could only "find" works the ledger or the chaining check had already verified: their more than 1,200 unlabelled rows were never read, so round yields are lower bounds, and the Semantic Scholar yield for the larger set was taken as zero because its rows were not stored. The intervals show that 20 to 29 positives are too few to judge a signal, not that the signals are equal; how many positives would be enough was not worked out. The 5% threshold and the top-200 depth were taken as given and not varied. Point 4's per-kind rule was derived from the order check and not itself simulated on a second data set. The delay between a round and its verification was not measured. The capture-recapture result holds for these two arms, whose seeds come from the keyword round; independent arms were not available to test.

## SW14 — The protocol is frozen and hashed before the first search; audit hashes are taken over a canonical form; ties are broken by a stable identifier

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Measured in `.local/quantum-determinism-2026-09-20/` (`protocol.md`, `run.py`, `result.json`, `result.md`), with no request and no model call. **Date:** 2026-09-20.

**Context:** Item 13 of the workflow review: the protocol is frozen before the search starts, every model call's input, output and hash are stored, and code gives the same decision on the same input in every run. The product already stores each model step's full input before the call and its output after, echoes `skill_package_hash` in every StepInput, records a model mismatch without using it, keeps provider payloads, and stores the compiled queries with the plan step. It has no single frozen protocol record for a research, and the migrations show no hash column for a step's input or output (only `skill_package_hash` and file and passage text hashes; checked in `storage/migrations` and `workflow/store.py`, not further). Measured: five code stages of the earlier measurements (four ranking signals with RRF, code-selected seeds and 200 seeded draws; the abstract code stage; title pairs and the merge rule over 1,009 DOIs; the code gate on 78 full texts; status and reason code of 160 works) were run in separate processes under `PYTHONHASHSEED` 1 and 2 and with input rows shuffled twice. Compared by canonical form (rows sorted by identifier, unordered pairs, sorted keys), all nine output files were identical in all four conditions: no decision changed. Compared by raw bytes, one file differed between hash seeds (titles held in a `set`, so equal-similarity pairs are listed in another order) and six differed under shuffled rows (row order, or the order of equal ranks, follows the input). In the product, `compile_queries`, `chunk_page` and `fuse_rankings` gave the same output under both hash seeds; `fuse_rankings` breaks a score tie by which ranking is passed first and by row position.

**Decision:**

1. Before the first provider request a research stores one protocol record: question and inclusion criterion with its parts, concept blocks and vocabulary, claim words and exclusion words, enabled arms and providers, signal list, thresholds and the rule table version, model and effort per step, method package hash and code version. The record is hashed and every later step row carries that hash.
2. A change to any of it opens a new revision with a reason (the existing scope revision mechanism); it is never edited in place. Decisions made under an earlier revision keep their revision (SW11 point 10).
3. Every model call stores input, output, the hash of each, the requested and the answering model, and the protocol hash. Every provider request stores the payload and its hash. A decision is reproduced from stored payloads, never from a new live search.
4. Hashes are taken over a canonical form: rows sorted by a stable identifier, keys sorted, pairs unordered. A hash over raw output bytes is not used for audit, because it reports differences that are not differences.
5. "Same input" means the same stored payloads, the same protocol hash and the same code version. The promise of repeatability covers code only; a model step is auditable because its input and output are stored, not because it repeats (SW1 point 6).
6. Every ordering that feeds a decision or a cut breaks ties by a stable record identifier, not by input or argument order. Sets are not iterated where order can reach an output.
7. A replay check belongs to the test suite when this is implemented: the code stages run twice under different hash seeds and row orders and the canonical hashes must match.

**Limits:** Two hash seeds and two shuffles on one machine, one Python and one set of library versions. No embedding was recomputed (stored vectors were reused), so floating-point differences of an embedding model across machines were not seen; SQLite FTS tie order in the product was not tested. The measured scripts are the review's isolated stages, not product code, apart from the three product functions named above. That no decision changed does not show the stages are free of order dependence in general, only on this data; the tie behaviour of `fuse_rankings` is an example that did not surface. Points 1 to 3 and 7 were not built or tried, and the content list of the protocol record is a working list.

## SW15 — The model proposes the inclusion criterion, its parts and the cue phrases from the question; the phrases rank passages from the start, and the code gate closes nothing until it is calibrated on verified records

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Generalises SW1 point 5 and SW12 points 3 and 4, whose gate and cue list were written by hand for the quantum topic. Measured in `.local/generalized-criterion-2026-09-20/` (`protocol.md`, `run.py`, `result.json`, `result.md`; exploratory `consensus.py`, `v2.py`). **Date:** 2026-09-20.

**Context:** The owner's point: the quantum topic is one example, and for any research code and the model must arrive at the criterion and its words together. No entry said how the inclusion criterion, its parts (SW11 point 4), the claim words (SW12 point 3) and the strict-cue gate (SW1 point 5) come into existence for a new question. Measured: `deepseek-flash` (product DeepSeek connection, effort high, no tools, 7 calls of 7,800 to 11,900 tokens) saw only the question and returned a one-sentence criterion, 2 to 5 parts and 6 to 15 phrases per part, each marked strong or not; the hand-written lists were never shown. Code compiled every phrase to a literal pattern and applied one topic-independent rule. All runs gave a checkable criterion, also for a question from another field (school mindfulness trials: randomisation, programme, participants, anxiety outcome, validated scale, with scale names and 22 exclusion title words); that output was read, not measured. Passage rank on the 53 formulation papers of SW12 (a chunk holding a verified quote among the first 6 of its paper; hand list 49, BM25 plus embedding 20): the three runs gave 34, 36 and 47; two of them had turned topic aspects into parts. With one sentence added to the prompt (topic and setting are left to the search blocks; parts cover only what the paper must contain) the runs gave 42, 50 and 50, and the 29 phrases proposed in at least two of three runs gave 48. Phrase sets of two runs overlap little (Jaccard 0.16 to 0.28); the 11 phrases common to all three first runs are mostly the hand list. Dropping phrases that occur in none of the full texts changed nothing. Gate on the 78 full texts (`code_yes` when every part has a hit, a strong phrase hits and there are at least 5 hits): in all six runs it put 5 to 16 of the 19 works without a formulation into `code_yes`, against none for the hand gate; the model marked 35 to 78% of its phrases strong, and heuristic papers also write "maximize", "constraints" and "objective function".

**Decision:**

1. For every research the model proposes, from the question alone, the inclusion criterion in one sentence, its parts, cue phrases per part and exclusion title words. Topic and setting belong to the concept blocks of the search (SW2, SW3) and are not criterion parts; parts say only what the paper itself must contain.
2. The proposal is run three times and code keeps the phrases proposed in at least two runs. A single run is not used: its list varies too much.
3. The user sees criterion, parts and phrases in one step with the vocabulary confirmation of SW2 point 6, and can edit them. The confirmed version is part of the frozen protocol (SW14 point 1); a change opens a revision.
4. The confirmed phrases are used from the first record on for everything that orders and nothing that decides: criterion passages (SW12 point 3), the passages the full-text model reads, the cue sentences on "no" rows of the human queue, and the pages sent to Marker (SW12 point 4).
5. At the start of a research the code gate closes nothing. Every full text goes to the model proposal with verbatim quote verification by code and the two-run rule (SW1 points 1 and 6). Decision authority stays with code; only the saving of SW1 point 5 is postponed.
6. The gate is switched on per research once enough verified records of both kinds exist (works meeting the criterion and in-scope works that do not), and only with a rule that, on those records, closes no work of the second kind. The rule and the records it was checked on are stored with the protocol revision. If no such rule exists for a criterion, the gate stays off and the research runs on model proposals and the human queue.
7. The strength mark the model gives a phrase is not used.

**Limits:** One topic against a reference, one model, and the reference labels are not human-approved. The second prompt was written after seeing the first result and scored on the same data, so 42, 50, 50 and 48 are exploratory; the first prompt's 34 to 47 are the protocol figures. The targets are the verified quotes of the earlier adjudication, so the circularity noted in SW12 applies. Point 6 was not tried: how many verified records are enough, what form the calibrated rule takes and how many works it then closes are unknown, and so is the extra model cost of point 5 before the gate opens. Whether a user can judge and correct proposed parts was not measured, nor was any criterion without typical wording. The three-run consensus was measured for ranking only.

## SW16 — The code gate is off by default and is never learned automatically; it opens only with a rule the user approved and that made no false close on held-out verified records

**Status:** accepted as design direction in the workflow review with the owner; not implemented. Narrows SW15 point 6 and the general use of SW1 point 5. Measured in `.local/gate-calibration-2026-09-20/` (`protocol.md`, `run.py`, `result.json`, `result.md`), with no request and no model call and no deviation from the protocol. **Date:** 2026-09-20.

**Context:** SW15 point 6 let the gate open per research once a rule closed no negative on the verified records; whether code can find such a rule was not tried. Measured on the 70 quantum full texts with a reference label (51 with a formulation, 19 in scope without one): from n randomly drawn verified records code learned, among rules that close no negative, the one closing the most positives (one condition or the AND of two; "phrase occurs at least t times", t in 1, 2, 3, 5, or "at least k distinct phrases occur", k in 3, 5, 8, 12), and the rule was scored on the remaining records, 300 draws per n. With the 29-phrase consensus list of SW15's second prompt the learned rule closed a median 56 to 58% of the held-out positives, but it closed at least one held-out negative in 93% of the draws at n = 10, 88% at 20, 73% at 30 and 57% at 40 (27%, 23%, 17% and 12% of all held-out negatives). Tightening every threshold by one step lowered this to 56%, 36%, 21% and 8% of the draws while the positives closed fell to 38%, 35%, 29% and 15%. With the 38-phrase list of the first prompt 78 to 97% of the draws had a false close, and 61 to 88% after tightening. The hand gate of SW1 point 5 closed 53% of the positives and no negative on the same held-out sets. The best error-free rule on all 70 records was `"constraints" >= 1 AND distinct phrases >= 8` (30 positives); tightened, it closes 5.

**Decision:**

1. In the general workflow the code gate is off. Every full text goes to the model proposal with verbatim quote verification by code and the two-run rule (SW1 points 1 and 6, SW15 point 5). Never opening the gate is a normal outcome, not a failure.
2. Code does not learn a gate rule and switch it on by itself. A rule that is error-free on 10 to 40 verified records is not error-free on the rest.
3. A gate opens only when the user has seen and approved the rule and the rule closed no in-scope negative on verified records of that research that were not used to write or tune it. The rule, the records it was checked on and the approval are stored with the protocol revision (SW14 points 1 and 2).
4. Works a gate closes are part of the audit sample of SW11 point 8, and one false close found there switches the gate off for that research.
5. The run budget is planned without the gate's saving: two model calls per full text outside agreement cases, with the cue phrases of SW15 point 4 keeping each call to the selected passages. The 43% token saving of SW1 is a figure of the hand gate on this topic, not an expectation.

**Limits:** One topic, 19 negatives, and reference labels that are not human-approved. Only one rule family and one selection criterion (largest coverage among error-free rules) were tried; a fixed structure like the hand gate's with learned thresholds, selection by margin, more than 40 calibration records and another topic were not. The hand gate's zero is in-sample, since its thresholds were chosen on this data (SW1 Limits): the measurement shows that the learner is unsafe, not that a hand-written rule is safe on another topic. How many held-out records point 3 needs before an approval means something was not worked out, and point 4's switch-off was not tried. The cost of running without a gate was not measured for a whole research.
